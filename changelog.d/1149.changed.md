- **`datetime` now retains the named IANA zone on a value, so wall-clock
  arithmetic re-applies that zone's DST rule** (I#1149,
  decisions/stdlib/1149-named-zone-retention.md). A `DateTime` produced by
  `astimezone(dt, zone("…"))` carries its zone identity (an internal `zone_id`
  handle), so `replace(dt, …)` and `add(dt, duration)` now re-resolve the UTC
  offset at the new wall-clock time instead of keeping the stale one. `add` is
  pure-Python wall-clock arithmetic: it advances the naive wall-clock fields and
  re-resolves the offset (days and sub-day units alike — `timedelta(days=1) ==
  timedelta(seconds=86400)`), not a UTC-instant shift. Example: local noon on
  2024-03-09 in `America/New_York` is `-05:00`; `replace(day=10)` and
  `add(seconds(86400))` both yield `-04:00` (previously `replace` kept `-05:00`).
  Fixed-offset and naive values are unchanged (advancing the naive wall clock
  equals the old instant math and the offset does not re-resolve), so all prior
  naive/fixed behaviour is byte-for-byte identical. Non-uniform calendar units
  (months/years) stay out of core — they become dateutil `relativedelta` in the
  non-core datetime-utils package.
