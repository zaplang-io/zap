- **Borrowed-bytes `bytesview` — a non-owning view over `bytes`** (I#1082, PR#1106,
  decisions/lang/1106-bytesview.md). The `bytes` counterpart of `strview` (#1101):
  a non-owning view over an existing `bytes` value's storage with **zero heap
  allocation and zero copy** for read-only consumers. **Create** with `b.view()`
  (whole value) or `b.view(lo, hi)` / `b.view(lo)` — bounds are **byte** indices
  (I#337), matching `bytes` slicing — and sub-view with `v.view(lo, hi)`.
  **Consumers:** `v in d` / `d.get(v[, default])` on a `bytes`-keyed dict (no
  transient key), `v == b` / `v == w`, `len(v)` / `v.len()` (byte count),
  `v.__hash__()`, `v.startswith(...)` / `endswith` / `contains` (argument `bytes`
  or `bytesview`), and `v.to_bytes()` to materialize an owned `bytes`. Like
  `strview` it is affine (`ZAP5135`): a `bytesview` may not be a struct field,
  module global, collection element / dict key-or-value, or function return type;
  a bare local or a parameter is sound (the view carries its backing as a traced
  pointer, rewritten on a moving-GC evacuation via the #586 aggregate-root pairs).
  Experimental (docs/STABILITY.md); adding the type reserves the `bytesview`
  keyword. `bytes[a:b]` slice semantics are unchanged.
